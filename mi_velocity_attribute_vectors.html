Non-training attribute control: velocity-space version

Mixed result · quality works even better than the hidden-state version · camera's "motion" signal is an artifact, not real panning · no training, no LoRA

Same generalized method as the hidden-state attribute-vector page -- prompt-contrastive pairs across 3 source scenes, averaged into one direction, tested on a held-out scene's neutral prompt -- but steering a different locus: the transformer's consolidated noise_pred (the actual velocity prediction the scheduler uses to step the latents) instead of 20 separate per-block hidden states. Per Michael's suggestion in Slack: "have you also looked at the velocity components? That might be a simpler place to steer from."

Method: for each of 3 source scenes (duck-falls-in-box, potato-in-water, domino-in-juice), run base H3 twice with the identical seed/reference, changing only the prompt (clause A vs B). At 7 denoising steps, record the real noise_pred (mean over rows) for each run, diff (A−B), average over all steps and scenes into one [channels] direction -- no LoRA, no trained checkpoint, construction included. Apply it (at a few fixed scales in one job, given the earlier lesson about magnitude) to block_state.noise_pred after every real denoiser call, on a fresh base H3 generating the same held-out scene (paper-fall-water, neutral prompt) used throughout this investigation.

Headline finding: velocity-space steering is meaningfully better-behaved than hidden-state steering for this attribute. Quality works cleanly at full, unscaled magnitude -- something the hidden-state version could only achieve after scaling its vector down by 5-50x to avoid an outright noise collapse. Brightness also has a real effect with more headroom (0.1x is visible and intact, vs. hidden-state's narrower ~0.02x window) before it saturates at 1.0x. This is consistent with Michael's intuition that the consolidated velocity signal is a "simpler" locus than 20 separate per-block directions built from independently-diverging generations.
But camera and gravity are still not wins. Gravity shows no measurable effect at any tested scale, in either locus -- this specific attribute (fall vs. float) doesn't appear to produce a usable direction from this prompt-contrastive, cross-scene method at all, regardless of where you intervene. Camera's elevated "motion" reading at full scale looked promising in the metrics table alone, but the frame-by-frame check below shows it's a flickering color artifact in the water's reflection, not an actual pan -- exactly the kind of metric-vs-reality mismatch the methodology correction on the hidden-state page was meant to catch, so it's called out rather than reported as a success.
