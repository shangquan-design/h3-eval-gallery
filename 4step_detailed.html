D0 — Overview

This page compares our 4-step DMD checkpoint against our 6-step and previous-best 8-step checkpoints, plus two external 8-step references (LightX2V Turbo8, OpenVDN VDN-H3), across the same 14 held-out prompts / seeds / resolution / frame count / audio used throughout this project. The 4-step column was freshly generated for this comparison (Phase 5 gate450 full-14 eval, job 4803); the other four columns are reused byte-for-byte from stored eval clips. Rows stay time-synchronized; every video starts muted with a per-row picker to unmute exactly one.

Bottom line: gate450 is a validated 4-step candidate with quality comparable to the 6-step model. Its speed advantage is still unresolved: the current CPU-offload benchmark cannot distinguish step-count scaling reliably, so there is no measured basis yet for preferring 4-step over 6-step.
The tie/win/loss judgments below are an AI screening pass (full-video review by the assistant, not a human evaluation), reported from h3_sft_dmd/PHASE5_GATE450_FULL14_AND_BENCHMARK.md. They cover only our 4-step vs. our 6-step. The previous-best 8-step, Turbo8 and VDN-H3 columns are shown for side-by-side viewing only — no tie/win/loss claim is made against them.

D1 — Quality result

14/14 ties, 0 wins, 0 losses -- our 4-step (gate450) vs our 6-step (gate350). No new failure modes outside the 8-prompt hard-case gate board. Preserved vs. the 6-step checkpoint:

  • water-balloon rupture (n_water_balloon_splash)
  • campfire/background richness (s_campfire_sparks)
  • train-window camera-motion/parallax (h_train_window)
  • glass shatter, demolition, smoke
  • clean h_argument at the final checkpoint (with a transient +50 drift that recovered by +75 and was proven isolated to this one prompt/seed)

h_argument caveat: the 4-step checkpoint drifted to anime style at the +50 training gate, recovered by +75, and is clean at the final (+100) gate shown here. A dedicated isolation probe (three new two-person-interaction prompts run on the drifted checkpoint) rendered all three fully photorealistic, confirming the drift was specific to h_argument's exact prompt+seed, not a category-level regression. The 6-step checkpoint had zero drift across all its gates.

D2 — Speed & VRAM

Matched benchmark: exclusive dedicated node (--exclusive, dedicated GPU), same offload harness, one python process per arm, sequential, one cold-start measurement per arm.

ArmDenoising stepsE2E latencyPeak VRAM
Our 4-step (gate450)4183.67 s70.29 GB
Our 6-step (gate350)6190.29 s70.29 GB
Our prev. best 8-step (gate250)8176.93 s70.29 GB
LightX2V Turbo8 (corrected)8180.95 s70.29 GB

This offload-bound harness CANNOT resolve the E2E latency difference between 4/6/8-step checkpoints. A least-squares fit of E2E = fixed + steps*per_step over our (4,6,8)-step arms gives a physically-impossible NEGATIVE per-step slope; all four arms land in a ~13s band with no ordering by step count (the 8-step arm was 'fastest' this run). A second same-purpose benchmark job put the numbers in an entirely different absolute range and disagreed on 6-vs-8 ordering. The fixed overhead (~180s: CPU-offload component streaming + text encode + VAE + audio decode) dominates; removing 2-4 DiT forward passes (~4-8s) is below single-run measurement noise, and it can't be averaged out (repeated in-process calls OOM).

Our 4-step has NO demonstrable E2E speed advantage in this harness. The theoretical compute reduction (4 vs 8 = 50% fewer transformer forward passes) is real and would matter in an optimized serving stack that amortizes the fixed overhead, but cannot be shown here. Peak VRAM is identical for all four arms. This is a research harness, not a production-serving benchmark.

Not a denoise-only or production-serving benchmark. Turbo8's number is included because it runs through this same offload harness; VDN-H3 was not re-measured here.
