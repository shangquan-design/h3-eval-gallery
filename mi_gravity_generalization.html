Gravity attribute vector: 20-scene generalization check

1 confirmed positive case out of 20 · scale 0.25x for both loci

Follow-up to the hidden-state and velocity-space attribute-vector pages. After feedback that the original full-magnitude test was too distorted to judge fairly, both saved gravity vectors (no reconstruction) were scaled down to 0.25x and applied to 20 held-out scenes -- none used in building either vector. Dense-frame-checked, not sparse screenshots, per the methodology correction on the hidden-state page.

Headline finding: one real, confirmed case. mug-breaks is a base-H3 failure where the grabber tool never releases the mug for the entire clip. With the velocity gravity vector (×0.25) applied, dense 14-frame sampling shows the grabber releasing and the mug falling to the ground in the last ~10% of the clip -- something the unsteered baseline never does at all. This is the first scene, across all 20 tested (the 4 from the earlier pass plus these 16), where either locus produces a confirmed behavioral change that looks like "more gravity," not just an artifact.
One scene went the other way. double-cradle (a Newton's cradle release) shows both loci collapsing the baseline's dynamic swinging into near-total stillness -- optical-flow motion drops ~70% and the grabber doesn't visibly release at all in the sampled frames. That's motion being suppressed, not gravity being strengthened -- the opposite of what we're looking for, flagged here rather than quietly dropped.
Everything else (18/20 scenes): the same pattern as the first pass. Hidden-state is a near-constant brightness/blur shift, now much milder at 0.25x but still not behaviorally different from baseline. Velocity stays close to baseline with no clear directional effect. One scene (solid-ball-peakaboo) has an unusually dark baseline rendering that the hidden-state variant happens to brighten back to normal -- a coincidence of that scene's lighting, not a gravity effect (the lower/lift sequence and timing are otherwise identical).
